Beyond the answer
Beyond Marks explores the reasoning behind an answer. This is a walkthrough of the real application using demonstration activity, not classroom outcome data.

Investigate the reasoning
The learner questions a flawed explanation, selects the faulty segment, and explains their thinking. Confidence and context are optional, and remain learner reports.

Keep possibilities open
Conceptual difficulty, misreading, time pressure, and unfinished work remain competing explanations. Evidence weights are not probabilities of ability. The learner can disagree.

One useful next question
A report of time pressure leads to a fresh, unhurried probe. Distinct questions test different possibilities, while acknowledging when one answer cannot distinguish them.

Support with purpose
A short explanation and worked example lead to an active prediction. Opening a lesson does not count as completing it.

Apply the idea elsewhere
The transfer challenge asks the learner to repair code in a new setting. The answer and selected reasoning are checked together.

Check independently
Independent verification removes earlier hints and asks for two results. A failed check returns to fresh support and a different verification task.

Challenge the rule
Reflection asks for a counterexample and a better rule. Learners can pause, resume, or ask their teacher to review the evidence.

Return after a real delay
Successful reflection schedules fresh checks after seven and thirty days. These checks are scheduled, not completed. Retained understanding can only be tested after the real delay.

Preserve the learning story
The teacher timeline keeps responses, interpretations, disagreement, and learner context visible. Written explanations stay available for human review alongside the structured assessment.

Keep teachers in control
Teachers can review AI feedback and resolve student disputes without erasing the original assessment. This working prototype still needs a real classroom pilot.
